Methodology, reliability & source notebook

Historical data, not monthly interpolation

The browser requests March 1–April 30 for each year from 2016 through 2025 using Open-Meteo’s historical API with the ERA5 model explicitly selected. This is a ten-year descriptive sample, not a 30-year climate normal or measured trend. ERA5 is ECMWF/Copernicus reanalysis combining observations and atmospheric modeling.

No weekly research values are bundled: direct archive access was unavailable in the development environment on 2 October 2026. Successful browser downloads are aggregated locally; unavailable records stay missing. The old monthly summaries are not used as weekly evidence.

Exact weekly calculation

Within each month, use days 1–7, 8–14, 15–21, 22–28 and 29–month end. Dates and daily boundaries are UTC, not local ski-day boundaries. For each year and period, average temperatures and hourly snow depth; sum snowfall, rain and precipitation; count snow and rain days. Then take the arithmetic mean of the complete yearly periods, with equal weight per year.

A metric needs every expected daily value (and all 24 hourly values per day for depth) to contribute a year. Otherwise that year is excluded for that metric only. Coverage is displayed and exported; values with different year coverage are not directly comparable. Zero is retained; missing data is never zero-filled. Week 5 totals cover only two or three days.

Snow, rain and temperatures

Snow depth is mean modeled grid-cell snow depth, converted from metres to centimetres—not a resort-reported base depth. Fresh snowfall is accumulated new snow, not settled depth. A snow day has at least 1 cm of modeled snowfall; a rain day has at least 1 mm of liquid rain. Both can occur on the same day.

Daily maximum and minimum temperatures are averaged separately. Rain totals exclude snowfall; total precipitation includes snow’s water equivalent. The API’s modeled precipitation-phase partition and snow-density assumptions add uncertainty: do not infer observed rain/snow ratios or piste conditions.

Mountain-scale limitations

Requests use approximate locality reference coordinates, not weather stations or summit points. ERA5 has an approximately 0.25° (~25 km) grid; nearby resorts can share a cell. Automatic elevation downscaling is disabled (elevation=nan) to retain grid conditions. Returned coordinates and elevations are listed below and in the CSV.

Terrain, altitude, aspect, snowmaking and grooming are not resolved at piste scale. This cannot rank ski-area snow reliability, establish lift openings or assess avalanche safety. For local observed conditions, consult Météo-France station and mountain observations.
