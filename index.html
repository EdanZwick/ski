A substantial reporting spread

Published April base-depth averages range from 26 cm in Morzine to 86 cm in La Tania and La Plagne. Reporting elevations and samples are not standardized: this is not a like-for-like snow-reliability ranking. Snow sources

02 / MAY CAUTION

No report ≠ no snow

We could not verify comparable May base-depth, snowfall or snowfall-day summaries for these five areas. Do not infer bare mountains—or open lifts—from missing reports. Coverage limitations

Methodology, reliability & source notebook

What “historical” means here

This is a comparison of published historical summaries, not an analysis of downloaded daily station observations. Research date: 2 October 2026. Direct source-page and archive-API access was unavailable in the research environment; values were recovered from source-specific web-search results. Indexed snapshots can lag the live pages. Treat the results as a preliminary, secondary-source comparison.

We do not claim a verified common 30-year climate normal, a fixed sample size, or a measured trend. No daily records were downloaded or independently re-aggregated. Unknown baselines and measurement elevations limit comparability.

Snow depth is not snowfall

Base depth is OnTheSnow’s published average reported base snow depth, not upper-mountain depth or an end-of-month value. The reporting elevation is not verified. Fresh snowfall is the published monthly snowfall average, not settled snowpack depth. Snow days is the provider’s snowfall-day average; a consistent event threshold and reporting coverage could not be verified.

Snow reports can be affected by operating calendars, observation practices, grooming and snowmaking. April averages may cover only part of the month. No verified May snow values are substituted with zeros.

One weather provider; baseline unverified

All five areas use Weather and Climate’s published average daytime and nighttime temperatures, precipitation and rainy/wet days. We retain the provider’s whole-degree and whole-millimetre precision. We do not calculate an average daily mean from daytime and nighttime values.

The exact historical reference period for these monthly values could not be confirmed. Station provenance, coordinates and representative elevations are also unverified. Using one provider does not establish equivalent geographic reference points or a quality-controlled common climate normal.

Rain limitation: the precipitation figures include rain and snow’s liquid-water equivalent. A separate rain-only total was not verified. Rainy/wet-day thresholds and treatment of mixed precipitation are unverified. Weather temperatures describe the source’s named locality, not a verified village altitude or upper slopes.

What this cannot establish

These summaries are not forecasts, avalanche assessments, ski-opening calendars, or proof of skiable terrain. Cross-provider snow and weather figures must not be combined to infer a rain-to-snow ratio. Snow days and rain days can overlap; neither is a count of days with snow lying on the ground.

For a decision-grade follow-up, obtain a common period of quality-controlled daily station records from Météo-France open data, document each station’s elevation and completeness, and use mountain snow observations or an explicitly labeled reanalysis for off-season snow coverage.
